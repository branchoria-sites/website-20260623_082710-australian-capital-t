## Introduction

The northern Tasmanian sighting wave centred on Launceston is often presented as one of the state's most notable UFO [clusters]({{ 'clusters/' | relative_url }}). Yet the official record tells a more cautious story. When Australian Department of Air files are examined, many of the reports that helped create the impression of a concentrated UFO wave were accompanied by ordinary probable causes. Rather than treating the sightings as evidence of a single unexplained phenomenon, officials frequently linked them to astronomical objects, atmospheric effects, balloons, aircraft and even birds. Those [explanations]({{ 'explanations-0349ac/' | relative_url }}) did not erase the reports, but they substantially weakened the idea that the Launceston cluster represented a coherent mystery. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b8/Investigations_of_flying_saucers_in_Australia%2C_A703%2C_569-5-451.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons-c----r;:-April 22, 2013 — OF liiR, Ct&quot;&quot;U!ERRA, ACT, FROM 1960. Brief Description of aerial Sighting. Twin Yellow Lights...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2013</span></span></span>

## What the Department of Air listed for 1960

The key document is a Department of Air summary of unusual aerial sightings reported during 1960. [Northern Tasmania]({{ 'northern-wave/' | relative_url }}) appears repeatedly in the list. Entries include twin yellow lights over Launceston in January, a crescent-shaped object near Launceston in October, the well-known [Cressy]({{ 'cressy/' | relative_url }}) report, later sightings around Longford, Poatina and Trevallyn, and several other observations across the region. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b8/Investigations_of_flying_saucers_in_Australia%2C_A703%2C_569-5-451.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons-c----r;:-April 22, 2013 — OF liiR, Ct&quot;&quot;U!ERRA, ACT, FROM 1960. Brief Description of aerial Sighting. Twin Yellow Lights...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2013</span></span></span>

At first glance, the concentration of reports appears impressive. However, the same summary also records official assessments of likely causes. Instead of treating the sightings as a connected series of unexplained events, investigators often assigned conventional explanations. Among the causes listed were:

## Birds, balloons, aircraft and astronomical explanations

Several of the official explanations directly challenge the more dramatic interpretations of the Launceston wave.

### Astronomical objects

A number of Tasmanian sightings were attributed to astronomical causes. Bright planets, stars near the horizon and other celestial objects can appear surprisingly unusual, especially when viewed through haze, cloud layers or changing atmospheric conditions. The Department of Air summary assigned an astronomical explanation to several sightings associated with northern Tasmania. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/AustralianUFOFiles/PP959-1_5-3-AIR_1826380_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Australian UFO Files&quot;... This matters because many reports described bright stationary or slowly moving lights rather than structured craft observed at close range. ### Refraction and atmospheric effects

The files also refer to refraction as a probable cause in some cases. Atmospheric refraction can distort the apparent position, colour and movement of lights. Over water, valleys or changing temperature layers, distant lights can seem to hover, shift direction or alter shape. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b8/Investigations_of_flying_saucers_in_Australia%2C_A703%2C_569-5-451.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia Commons-c----r;:-April 22, 2013 — OF liiR, Ct&quot;&quot;U!ERRA, ACT, FROM 1960. ### Balloons

One entry in the official summary was linked to a meteorological balloon carrying a light. Weather balloons were common aviation tools and could appear highly unusual to observers unfamiliar with them. Their slow movement, changing altitude and reflective surfaces often matched features reported in UFO accounts. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/AustralianUFOFiles/PP959-1_5-3-AIR_1826380_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Internet ArchiveFull text of &quot;Australian UFO Files&quot;... ### Aircraft and birds

The files also include references to conventional aircraft, including a probable TAA Viscount airliner, as likely explanations. Northern Tasmania lay beneath established aviation routes, making aircraft a routine part of the local sky. ## What the Launceston case shows about UFO waves

The Launceston cluster is a useful example of how UFO waves can change when official records become available.
