## Introduction

Some of the most dramatic “UFO waves” reported in and around the [Northern Territory]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-northern/' | relative_url }}) have a surprisingly modern cause: rocket launches. A rocket climbing into the upper atmosphere can produce enormous glowing clouds, spirals, expanding rings and jellyfish-shaped forms that look unlike ordinary aircraft, satellites or meteors. Because these displays can be visible across thousands of kilometres, a single launch can trigger dozens or even hundreds of reports from people who all see the same strange object at roughly the same time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

## Why rocket plumes can look so strange

The key to understanding these sightings is altitude. After launch, a rocket rapidly climbs above the lower atmosphere into regions where the air is extremely thin. Exhaust gases that would form a narrow trail near the ground can expand into a vast cloud hundreds of kilometres above Earth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

The most spectacular displays occur shortly before sunrise or after sunset. At ground level, observers are in darkness or deep twilight, but the rocket and its exhaust plume are still illuminated by sunlight high above the Earth. The result is a brilliant glowing cloud that can appear suspended in a dark sky. This effect is widely known as the “twilight phenomenon” or, more informally, a “space jellyfish”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

Several features make these events look unlike familiar aircraft:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Rapid shape changes:** expanding exhaust clouds can appear to grow, blossom or explode.
* **Unusual colours:** sunlight scattering through frozen exhaust particles can produce white, blue, pink or orange tones.
* **Spiral patterns:** fuel venting from a spinning upper stage can create luminous corkscrew shapes.
* **Silent movement:** the object is far too distant for sound to accompany what observers see.
* **Enormous apparent size:** the illuminated cloud may span a large fraction of the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

</div>

These characteristics closely match many classic UFO descriptions, especially reports involving glowing discs, spirals, expanding spheres or shape-shifting lights.

## What a sunlit rocket plume looks like

Rocket-related UFO reports tend to follow recognisable patterns.

A launch itself may first appear as a bright moving point with a luminous tail. Minutes later, when an upper stage ignites or vents propellant, the display can transform into a glowing cloud that expands dramatically. Witnesses often describe this stage as looking artificial, intelligent or unlike any known aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spacelaunchschedule.com/news/the-jellyfish-effect-during-rocket-launches-a-stunning-twilight-phenomenon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spacelaunchschedule.com">[Space Launch Schedule]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spacelaunchschedule.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Space Launch ScheduleRocket Launch Jellyfish Effect: A Guide to Twilight&#x27;s Best...This captivating visual phenomenon occurs when a rocke...</span></span></span>

In some cases, a circular or spiral structure develops. This happens when a rocket stage rotates while releasing excess fuel. The vented material freezes and reflects sunlight, producing a bright geometric pattern visible over vast distances. Similar spirals have generated UFO speculation in Australia, New Zealand, Britain and Europe before being traced to rocket operations. ## Why northern Australia can see one event widely

The Northern Territory is especially well placed to experience mass sightings from rocket plumes.

A high-altitude exhaust cloud may be illuminated hundreds of kilometres above the Earth. Unlike a low-level aircraft light, it is not limited to a local area. People in Darwin, Katherine, Alice Springs and remote communities can potentially observe the same phenomenon if viewing conditions are favourable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Twilight phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Twilight phenomenon</span></span></span>

Northern Australia also sits beneath routes where launches from overseas spaceports can become visible. Chinese launches, SpaceX missions and other orbital operations have repeatedly produced unusual sky displays visible from parts of Australia. Media reports have documented occasions when glowing plumes prompted widespread public speculation before astronomers linked them to known launches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/10NewsAU/posts/a-mysterious-white-plume-has-lit-up-the-night-sky-across-parts-of-australia-in-w/1434405662059321/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A mysterious white plume has lit up the night sky across...Astronomers have confirmed the phenomenon wasn&#x27;t weather-related, but...</span></span></span>

This broad visibility helps explain why rocket plumes can generate particularly convincing UFO episodes. When many independent witnesses report the same object, the event appears more credible. Yet in these cases, the large number of observers may result from the object's extraordinary altitude rather than from anything unexplained.

One well-known example occurred in June 2010 when a luminous spiral appeared before dawn across parts of eastern Australia. ## How to compare reports with launch notices

When a striking sky sighting occurs in the Northern Territory, several checks can quickly indicate whether a rocket plume is involved.

First, compare the time of the sighting with published launch schedules. Many commercial, government and military launches are publicly announced in advance. A launch occurring within the previous hour is a strong clue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-environment/2025/03/25/spiral-spacex-launch-mystery-blue/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Washington Post Did you see an alien-looking swirl in the sky?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It&#x27;s probably SpaceX.On the night of March 24, 2025, skywatchers across Europe, from Britain to Croatia, observed a mysterious light blue...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2025</span></span></span>
