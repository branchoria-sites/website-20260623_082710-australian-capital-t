## Introduction

One of the most enduring questions about the 1966 [Westall]({{ 'westall/' | relative_url }}) incident is not what students saw, but what happened afterwards. Former pupils have repeatedly claimed that, following the sighting, school authorities gathered students together and instructed them not to discuss the event. Over time, those memories transformed the Westall story from a mass sighting case into a debate about authority, secrecy and possible suppression.

The evidence for a formal cover-up is limited and largely based on later witness recollections rather than surviving official [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}). However, there is little dispute that school staff faced an unusually disruptive situation on the day itself. The central question is whether the response reflected an attempt to conceal something extraordinary or a principal's effort to restore order after hundreds of excited students had abandoned normal school routine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://localhistory.kingston.vic.gov.au/articles/528" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: localhistory.kingston.vic.gov.au">[Kingston Local History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">localhistory.kingston.vic.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kingston Local HistoryAn Ongoing Mystery: The Westall Flying Saucer Incident11 Jun 2012 — The headmaster, Mr Frank Samblebe, and his staf...</span></span></span>

## Frank Samblebe's assembly and school discipline

Accounts from former students consistently describe an assembly held after the sighting. According to these recollections, principal Frank Samblebe addressed the school and attempted to bring the situation under control. Witnesses interviewed decades later recalled being told that what had been seen was probably nothing unusual and that students should stop discussing it. Some remembered warnings that continued discussion could result in disciplinary consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/australian-broadcasting-corporation_witnesses-to-an-alleged-ufo-sighting-were-activity-7447417955736424448-S0nK" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: linkedin.com">[linkedin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">linkedin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Linked In Witnesses to an alleged UFO sighting were warned to keep</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Witnesses to an alleged UFO sighting were warned to keep...April 8, 2026 — The headmaster called General Assembly and he dictate...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2026</span></span></span>

The existence of an assembly is widely reported in later investigations of the case, but interpretations differ. To believers in a cover-up, the assembly represents the first step in suppressing witness testimony. To others, it looks like a predictable reaction from a school administrator confronted with a chaotic situation in which large numbers of students had left classes, rushed towards fences and nearby paddocks, and generated intense media interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://localhistory.kingston.vic.gov.au/articles/528" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: localhistory.kingston.vic.gov.au">[Kingston Local History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">localhistory.kingston.vic.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kingston Local HistoryAn Ongoing Mystery: The Westall Flying Saucer Incident11 Jun 2012 — The headmaster, Mr Frank Samblebe, and his staf...</span></span></span>

## Claims of official warnings and interviews

## Why the silence story became central to Westall

The reported instruction to keep quiet became one of the most memorable aspects of the case because it offered an explanation for another puzzle: why there appeared to be relatively little official discussion of an event supposedly witnessed by hundreds of people.

Contemporary newspaper coverage already hinted at this issue. One local headline described the affair as a "Flying Saucer Mystery" and noted that the school remained largely silent about what had occurred. That language helped establish an early connection between the sighting and the idea that information was being withheld.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blogs.slv.vic.gov.au/our-stories/strange-lights-in-the-sky-the-westall-ufo-event-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogs.slv.vic.gov.au">[State Library Victoria]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogs.slv.vic.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">strange lights in the sky the westall ufo event 1966</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State Library VictoriaStrange lights in the sky: The Westall UFO event, 19666 Apr 2024 — On 6 April 1966 something strange happened at a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1966</span></span></span>

## Suppression claim or ordinary school control?

The strongest argument for suppression is the consistency with which many former students remember being discouraged from discussing the sighting. Independent witnesses have repeated versions of that story across interviews conducted years apart.
